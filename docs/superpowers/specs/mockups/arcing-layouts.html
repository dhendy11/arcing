Arcing Layouts

Three ways to draw the same arc on the Relate screen, each at 1024 px. The sample is John Piper's own arc of Romans 12:1-2 from Biblical Exegesis, pp. 21-23: his four propositions, his labels, his circles. Nothing here is a new split or a new label.

The chrome around the arc is identical in all three so the only thing that differs is how the arc is drawn.

A. Paper style, turned vertical

Piper's conventions (thick ink arcs, symbols in bold inside the arcs, the negative as a stroke, the primary member circled) with the propositions stacked so the text can be read at full width. Arcs sit to the right, smallest nearest the text.

C. Booklet orientation

Exactly how Piper draws it on p. 23: the proposition numbers in a row, semicircular arcs stacked above them, and the propositions listed underneath by number. Genuinely different because the arc is read horizontally, the way the paper arcs you photograph on Saturdays are. The cost is that the text and the diagram are apart, so you read by number, and the row width caps how many propositions fit at 1024 px (Piper's Luke 12:35-38 on p. 24 has twelve).

Sources and what was not confirmed

  1. The sample arc. John Piper, Biblical Exegesis: Discovering the Meaning of Scriptural Texts, cdn.desiringgod.org/pdf/booklets/BTBX.pdf. Propositions and their numbering (12:1, 12:2a, 12:2b, 12:2c) are p. 21 verbatim; the finished diagram with the row "1 2a b c" is p. 23. The diagram reads, left to right: Pu circled over 1, a long stroke over 2a, + over b, Pu circled over c, Ac in the arc over 2a-b, Ac in the arc over 2a-c, and no symbol on the outer arc. Every label was legible at 300 dpi; nothing was guessed.
  2. One wording mismatch in the booklet itself. The text on p. 22 says "I circle the Pur", but the drawing on both p. 22 and p. 23 prints "Pu" inside the ellipse. A and C reproduce the drawing (Pu). B uses Biblearc's own abbreviation (Pur), which is what its picker shows.
  3. The negative symbol. Piper draws it as a long horizontal stroke. A and C draw a stroke; B and the palette use the minus sign, which is Biblearc's form (its picker lists it as a minus over a plus).
  4. Biblearc's layout, verified. The two module screenshots cited under B (published 2023 in a Biblearc blog post) show: propositions stacked in bordered cells, verse-letter numbers in gold at the left of the text, the translation name above the column, arcs to the right of the text, one arc per proposition nearest the text and each enclosing arc one step further out, labels inside the arcs coloured by group (blue restatement, red distinct statement, green coordinate), and the main member of every subordinate relationship on a light grey disc with a star on the bracket side. The same screenshots show a bracket view on the left of the text; that is Biblearc's Bracketing module and is left out here because bracketing is not in v1. Interaction, from the Advent 2020 course page (equip.biblearc.com/course/advent2020/63782): "Click on a word to have it begin a new proposition. Click an X on the left to rejoin two propositions." "Click where you would like the arc to begin and end. Indicate a relationship by clicking an arc's 'handle.'" "Click the double arrow to switch the order of a relationship."
  5. Not confirmed about Biblearc. Exact colours and typeface are sampled from the 2023 screenshots and may have changed. Biblearc marks a main member in every subordinate relationship, including Negative-Positive; Piper circles only in Ac-Pur, Ac-Res and Sit-R (p. 22), so B follows Piper's data and leaves the minus and plus unmarked. The lesson page given in the brief (course/arcing/130972) carries one image and it is the title page of a 2009 seminary paper, not the module, so it was not used.
  6. Symbols and definitions. Symbol forms follow Piper's chart, pp. 33-34, which also states that coordinate symbols are written between the arcs and subordinate symbols inside them. Group order and the eight distinct-statement rows follow the chart's order. The one-line definitions are quoted from Biblearc's cheat sheet "The 18 Logical Relationships" as recorded in references/personal/bible-arcing-primer.md.
  7. Numbering differs on purpose. A uses Piper's list labels (12:1, 12:2a...), C uses his diagram row (1, 2a, b, c) plus his list, B uses Biblearc's verse-letter style (1, 2a, 2b, 2c).
